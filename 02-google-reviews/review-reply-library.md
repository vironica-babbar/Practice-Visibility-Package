# Google Review Reply Library

Ready-to-paste replies to Google reviews that **never confirm or suggest that
the reviewer is a patient.** That's the HIPAA rule most practices break by
accident, usually while trying to be kind or to defend themselves.

**Where to paste:** Google Business Profile → **Reviews** → **Reply** under
the review. (You can also reach it by searching your practice name on Google
while signed in.)

| What | Limit / rule | Last checked | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reply length | Google doesn't publish an official limit. Third-party sources report about 4,000 characters. Every reply here is well under 400, and short is better. | 2026-09-23 | watch the editor |
| What you may not do | Offer incentives, ask only happy patients, discourage negative reviews, or have staff review you | 2026-09-23 | [Google Maps user-contributed content policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114) |
| Privacy | You may not disclose patient information in a reply, even if the reviewer shared it first | 2026-09-23 | HHS Office for Civil Rights enforcement actions, 2022 |

---

## The 6 golden rules (read before every reply)

1. **Don't confirm they're a patient.** Avoid "your visit," "your
   appointment," "when you came in," "your treatment," "your son," "your
   bill," "our records show."
2. **Don't repeat anything they said about their health**, even to sympathize.
3. **Don't use their name.** Google already shows it, and repeating it adds nothing.
4. **Don't argue or correct facts publicly.** Invite them to call instead.
5. **Reply once.** No back-and-forth threads.
6. **Wait until you're calm.** Have a second person read any reply to a
   negative review before you post it.

**Words to search for and delete before posting:** *visit, appointment,
treatment, procedure, prescription, diagnosis, therapy session, your child,
your baby, your son, your daughter, bill, insurance claim, records, chart,
when you came in, we remember you.*

## Choose your practice's approach

| Practice type | Recommended approach |
|---|---|
| OB/GYN, dermatology, pediatric | Reply to most reviews (positive and negative) using the templates below |
| Mental health, psychiatric, rehab | Many practices choose **not to reply to individual reviews at all**, because any reply can draw attention to a person's connection to behavioral health care. If you do reply, use only the "general" templates (A1, C1, D-General) and keep them short. Decide your policy once, write it down, and apply it to every review. |

It's always OK to not reply. A missing reply is never a HIPAA violation. A
careless one can be.

## A. Positive reviews

**A1: General (all practice types)**
```
Thank you for taking the time to share your feedback. We appreciate it, and we'll pass your kind words along to our team.
```

**A2: Warm (OB/GYN, dermatology, pediatric)**
```
Thank you so much for these kind words. Our team works hard to make [PRACTICE NAME] a welcoming place, and it means a lot to hear this.
```

**A3: Short**
```
Thank you for the kind review. We truly appreciate it.
```

**A4: Review names a provider or staff member**
```
Thank you for your kind words about our team. We'll be sure to share your feedback with them.
```
Don't repeat the provider's name. Naming them links the reviewer to that clinician.

> ❌ **Never write replies like these (fictional examples):**
> - "Congratulations on your new baby!" (OB/GYN)
> - "So glad your skin cleared up after your treatments!" (dermatology)
> - "Tell Max we said hi at his next checkup!" (pediatric)
> - "We're proud of your progress in our program." (rehab)

## B. Mixed reviews (some good, some bad)

**B1**
```
Thank you for sharing your feedback. We're glad to hear some things went well, and we take the concerns you raised seriously. We'd welcome the chance to learn more. Please call our office at [PHONE] and ask for [ROLE, e.g. our practice manager].
```

---

## C. Negative reviews: general

**C1: Works for any complaint, any practice type**
```
Thank you for sharing your feedback. Privacy laws prevent us from discussing any individual's care online, but we take all concerns seriously. We'd like to hear more directly. Please call our office at [PHONE] and ask for [ROLE, e.g. our practice manager].
```

**C2: Shorter**
```
We're sorry to hear about this experience. We take feedback seriously and would like to talk with you directly. Please call [PHONE] and ask for [ROLE].
```

> Note: "We're sorry to hear about this experience" doesn't confirm a visit.
> "We're sorry about your visit" does.

## D. Negative reviews: common topics

Each reply talks about **your policies in general**, not about the reviewer.

**D-Wait times**
```
Thank you for your feedback. We know everyone's time is valuable, and we are always working to keep schedules on track. We'd welcome the chance to hear more. Please call [PHONE] and ask for [ROLE].
```

**D-Scheduling or phone access**
```
Thank you for letting us know. We're always looking for ways to make it easier to reach us and schedule. We'd like to hear more about what happened. Please call [PHONE] and ask for [ROLE].
```

**D-Billing or insurance**
```
Thank you for your feedback. We can't discuss any account details online, but our billing team is glad to help with questions. Please call [PHONE] and ask for our billing office.
```

**D-Staff attitude or front desk**
```
Thank you for sharing this. We expect every member of our team to treat everyone with courtesy and respect, and we take this kind of feedback seriously. Please call [PHONE] and ask for [ROLE] so we can learn more.
```

**D-Unhappy with care or results**
```
Thank you for your feedback. Privacy laws prevent us from discussing anyone's care online. Our clinicians are committed to careful, individualized care, and we'd welcome the chance to talk with you directly. Please call [PHONE] and ask for [ROLE].
```

**D-Prescribing complaint (psychiatric, rehab)**
```
Thank you for sharing your feedback. We cannot discuss any individual's care online. Our clinicians make treatment decisions based on clinical guidelines and each person's needs. If you'd like to talk with us, please call [PHONE] and ask for [ROLE].
```

**D-General (the only negative reply mental health, psychiatric and rehab practices should use)**
```
Thank you for your feedback. We cannot discuss any individual's care online, but we take all concerns seriously. Please call [PHONE] if you'd like to talk with us.
```

## E. Tricky situations

**E1: The reviewer shares their own diagnosis or treatment details**

Use **C1** exactly. Don't acknowledge any of the health details they wrote.
> ❌ "We're sorry your IUD insertion was painful." This confirms a patient
> relationship and repeats health information.

**E2: The reviewer is a parent writing about a child (pediatric)**

Use **A1**, **C1** or a **D** reply. Never mention the child, the child's age
or the condition. Never write "your little one."

**E3: The reviewer is a family member (rehab, psychiatric)**
```
Thank you for reaching out. We cannot confirm or discuss whether any person has received services from us. If you have questions about our programs in general, please call [PHONE].
```
Substance use programs covered by **42 CFR Part 2** have stricter
confidentiality rules than HIPAA alone. Even confirming someone was in the
program can be a violation.

**E4: The review mentions self-harm, suicide or a crisis (any practice type)**

Take a screenshot for your clinical leadership first, then follow your
practice's safety protocol. Reply only with:
```
Thank you for sharing. If you or someone you know is in crisis, please call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline) or call 911 right away.
```
Don't discuss the person's situation publicly.

**E5: You think the review is fake, from a competitor or from someone who was never seen here**

- **Don't** reply "we have no record of you." That tells the public you
  checked your patient records.
- Report it instead: next to the review, click **⋮** (three dots) → **Report
  review**, and choose the reason that fits (for example, conflict of
  interest, off-topic or spam).
- If you want to reply while the report is reviewed, use **C1**.

**E6: The review contains threats, hate speech, profanity or another person's private information**

Don't reply. Report it (see E5). If there is a threat of violence, contact
local law enforcement.

**E7: A staff member, a relative or a former employee left a review**

Google's policy prohibits reviews based on a conflict of interest. Ask
current staff not to review the practice. Report reviews from former
employees (see E5).

## Copywriting specifics for review replies

The basics are in `00-start-here/copywriting-playbook.md`. These rules are
specific to replies.

| Topic | Rule | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Keywords** | None. Don't add "best [specialty] in [CITY]" or service names to replies. | It's a common "SEO tip" that looks spammy, adds outcome claims and doesn't help rankings |
| **Hyperlocal** | None. No neighborhoods, no hospitals, no provider names. | Local details can hint at where or by whom someone was treated |
| **Emojis** | None, even on 5-star reviews | Professional trust; screen readers read them aloud |
| **Exclamation marks** | At most one, and only on positive replies. None on negative replies. | Exclamation marks can sound dismissive on a complaint |
| **Tone** | Calm, brief, the same voice for every reviewer, never defensive | Patients read your replies to judge how you'd treat them |
| **Length** | Positive: 1–2 sentences. Negative: 2–4 sentences. | Long replies to complaints look like arguing |
| **Variety** | Rotate between the templates (A1–A4, C1–C2) so a page of replies doesn't look copy-pasted | Identical replies look automated |
| **Personalizing** | Personalize by **tone**, never by **detail**. Warmer or shorter is fine; mentioning what they wrote about is not. | Details are exactly what creates HIPAA risk |
| **Sign-off** | "— The team at [PRACTICE NAME]" or nothing | Personal names invite follow-up to that person |

### Reply timing

| Situation | When to reply |
|---|---|
| Positive review | Within about a week (batch them weekly) |
| Negative review | Wait at least 24 hours, have a second person read your draft, then reply within a few business days |
| Crisis or safety mention (E4) | Same day: follow your safety protocol first, then post the crisis-line reply |
| Mental health, psychiatric, rehab | Follow the written policy you chose (possibly "no replies"), and apply it the same way every time |

## Fill-in-the-blank reference

| Blank | Fill with | Example (fictional) |
|---|---|---|
| `[PHONE]` | main number from your fact sheet | (555) 010-0100 |
| `[ROLE]` | a role, not a person's name | our practice manager |
| `[PRACTICE NAME]` | from your fact sheet | Example Family Pediatrics |

**Tip:** don't sign replies with a staff member's personal name. Use the
practice name, or nothing at all.
