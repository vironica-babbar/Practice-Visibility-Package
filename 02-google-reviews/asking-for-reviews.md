# Asking for Google Reviews: Rules by Practice Type

Whether you may ask patients for reviews **depends on your practice type.**
Find your row, then read only your section.

| Practice type | May you ask patients for reviews? | Go to |
|---|---|---|
| OB/GYN clinic | Yes, following the rules below | Section 1 |
| Dermatology clinic | Yes, following the rules below | Section 1 |
| Pediatric clinic | Yes: ask the **parent or guardian**, never the child | Section 1 |
| Mental health clinic | **No**, not current clients (and, to be safe, not former clients) | Section 2 |

## Rules for everyone (all practice types)

- ❌ **No incentives.** No discounts, gift cards, raffles, donations "per
  review," free services or anything else in return for a review.
- ❌ **No cherry-picking.** Don't ask only the patients who seemed happy.
- ❌ **No "review gating."** Some reputation software asks "Were you
  satisfied?" first and only sends happy patients to Google. Google doesn't
  allow that. If your software does it, turn that feature off.
- ❌ **No scripts or star requests.** Don't say "please leave 5 stars" or
  suggest what to write.
- ❌ **No staff, family or friend reviews.** Also no reviews you write
  yourself or buy.
- ❌ **No pressure in the office.** Don't hand patients a tablet or ask them
  to post before they leave.
- ❌ **No Yelp requests.** Yelp prohibits asking anyone for reviews, in any form. Point requests to Google only.
- ✅ **Same request, same way, for everyone.** A standard message sent on a
  fixed schedule is the safest approach.

## Section 1: OB/GYN, dermatology and pediatric clinics

### How to ask safely

1. **Choose one or two standard methods** (templates below) and use them for
   every patient the same way, for example an email or text sent automatically
   2 days after every visit.
2. **Use contact details the patient already gave you**, and only text patients
   who have agreed to receive texts from your practice.
3. **Keep the message general.** Don't mention the visit reason, the
   provider seen, results or anything medical. A text can be read by whoever
   holds the phone.
4. **Get your review link:** in Google Business Profile, look for **Ask for
   reviews** or **Share review form** and copy the link. This is your
   `[REVIEW LINK]`.

**Pediatric clinics:** send requests only to the parent or guardian's
contact, never directly to a child or teen.

**OB/GYN clinics:** some visits involve difficult news (for example, pregnancy
loss). Many practices write a rule in advance that leaves certain **visit
types** out of automatic requests. Base the rule on visit type only, never on
whether a patient seemed satisfied, and have your compliance lead approve it.
The same idea can apply to other specialties.

### Template 1-A: Email (paste into your patient email or reminder system)

**Subject:**
```
A quick favor from [PRACTICE NAME]
```
**Body:**
```
Hello,

Thank you for choosing [PRACTICE NAME]. If you have a minute, we'd be grateful if you shared your honest feedback about our practice on Google. Your review helps others in [CITY] find care.

Leave a review: [REVIEW LINK]

Please don't include personal health information in your review, since reviews are public.

If you have a concern you'd like to share privately, please call us at [PHONE].

Thank you,
The team at [PRACTICE NAME]

To stop receiving these messages, reply STOP or call [PHONE].
```

> Why "please call us" isn't gating: it's offered to everyone in the same
> message, alongside the review link. Don't send different versions to
> different patients.

### Template 1-B: Text message (keep under 160 characters where possible)

```
[PRACTICE NAME]: Thank you for choosing us. We'd value your honest feedback on Google: [REVIEW LINK] Reply STOP to opt out.
```

### Template 1-C: Printed card at checkout or in the waiting room

```
Thank you for choosing [PRACTICE NAME].
We'd value your honest feedback on Google.
Scan the code or visit: [REVIEW LINK]
Please don't include personal health information. Reviews are public.
```
Print a QR code for `[REVIEW LINK]` on the card. Hand it to every patient,
or display it at the desk. Don't ask anyone to scan it while they're there.

### Template 1-D: Website footer line

```
Share your feedback about [PRACTICE NAME] on Google: [REVIEW LINK]
```

### Copywriting specifics for review requests

| Topic | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Wording** | Ask for "honest feedback," never "a great review" or "5 stars" |
| **Keywords** | Never suggest words to include ("mention our skin checks!"). Google forbids asking for specific content. |
| **Emojis** | None, including in texts. Some phones show them as boxes, and they make the message look like spam. |
| **Length** | Text: one sentence plus the link. Email: under 100 words. |
| **Personalizing** | Use no details from the visit: no provider name, no reason for visit |
| **How often** | Ask each patient **once**, after a visit, and not again for at least a year. Repeated requests feel like pressure. |
| **Timing** | 1–3 days after the visit, during daytime hours (for example, 10 AM – 6 PM local time) |
| **Language** | If a patient's preferred language isn't English, send the request in that language, using a professionally translated version |

## Section 2: Mental health, psychiatric and rehab practices

### The short version

**Don't ask clients or patients for reviews,** whether in person, by email,
by text, on a card or through software.

### Why

- **Ethics codes:** the APA Ethics Code (5.05) says psychologists don't
  solicit testimonials from current clients. The ACA Code of Ethics (C.3.b)
  says counselors don't solicit them from current **or former** clients. Other
  licenses (social workers, marriage and family therapists, psychiatrists,
  nurse practitioners) have their own codes and state board rules, and the
  same concern about pressure applies. **This package takes the cautious path for
  everyone.** Check your own license's code and state board.
- **Privacy:** a public review can reveal that someone is receiving mental
  health or addiction care. That can harm the person.
- **Rehab and addiction programs** covered by 42 CFR Part 2 have stricter
  confidentiality rules. Don't ask current patients, alumni or families.

### What you can do instead

- **Keep your Google profile complete and accurate** (see
  `01-google-business-profile`). A complete profile with correct hours,
  services and insurance helps people find you even with few reviews.
- **Put more effort into directories** where reviews matter less, such as
  Psychology Today, insurance directories and FindTreatment.gov (later
  modules in this package).
- **Passive link (optional):** a neutral line on your website that anyone can
  see, not sent to clients:
  ```
  Find [PRACTICE NAME] on Google: [GOOGLE PROFILE LINK]
  ```
  Use your profile link, not a "write a review" link.
- **Don't ask colleagues or referral partners for reviews.** Google treats
  professional relationships as a conflict of interest.

### If a client offers to write a review

A client may bring it up themselves. Here is a neutral response you can
adapt (have your supervisor or compliance lead approve it first):

```
Thank you, I appreciate that you'd think of it. Whether to write a review is entirely your choice, and I can't ask you to. If you do, please keep in mind that reviews are public and could show that you've received care here.
```

Then move on. Don't follow up about it.
