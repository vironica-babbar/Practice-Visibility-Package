# Website Visibility Guide

Your website is the one place online **you fully control**. Google, AI
assistants and directories all read it to check what you offer, where you are
and who works there. A small, accurate, well-organized site beats a big, vague
one.

This module covers **the pages that make you findable**: providers, services,
locations and insurance. It doesn't cover blogs, design, ads or analytics.

## Step 1: The pages every practice needs

Tick off what you have. Each page type has a template in `page-templates.md`.

| Page | One per… | Why it matters for visibility |
|---|---|---|
| **Home** | practice | Says who you are, what you do and where, in the first screen |
| **Provider page** | clinician | People search clinicians by name. Google and AI assistants use these pages to confirm who works where. |
| **Service page** | main service (3–6 to start) | Each page can show up for searches about that service, like "skin check Springfield" |
| **Location page** | real office | Gives Google and patients one clear page per address. Link your Google profile to it. |
| **Insurance & payment page** | practice | "Does [practice] take [insurance]?" is one of the most common patient questions |
| **New patients page** | practice | Tells patients whether you're accepting new patients and how to start |
| **Contact** | practice | Name, address and phone written **exactly** as in your fact sheet |

### Extra pages by practice type

| Practice type | Add these pages (only if true) |
|---|---|
| Mental health | "What to expect in your first session" · one page per main concern (anxiety, couples, teens) · "Fees & insurance," including out-of-network and superbills if you offer them |
| OB/GYN | Prenatal care, naming your delivering hospital · Gynecology · Your midwives or nurse practitioners (each gets a provider page) |
| Dermatology | **Separate** medical and cosmetic sections, each with its own insurance/payment info · Skin cancer screening |
| Pediatrics | Newborns and expecting parents (prenatal meet-and-greet, if offered) · Same-day sick visits · After-hours / nurse line |
| Psychiatric | What to expect at a psychiatric evaluation · Medication management · Telepsychiatry (list licensed states) · Practice policies (for example, how prescriptions and refills work) |
| Rehab / addiction | One page per level of care (detox, residential, IOP, outpatient) · Admissions process · For families · Licensing and accreditation |

### Pages you should NOT create

- ❌ **City pages for towns where you have no office** ("Pediatrician in
  Townville," "Pediatrician in Villageton," and so on). Google calls this
  **doorway abuse** and it can hurt your whole site. One page per **real**
  office only.
- ❌ **Near-copies for synonyms** (a "therapist" page, a "counselor" page and a
  "psychotherapist" page that all say the same thing). Make one page and use
  the synonyms naturally on it.
- ❌ **Mass-produced service pages** that are the same text with the
  condition name swapped. Google calls this scaled content abuse.

| Rule | Last checked | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Doorway abuse, scaled content abuse, keyword stuffing (including "blocks of text that list cities") | 2026-09-23 | [Google Search spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) |

## Step 2: Link it all together

- **Google Business Profile → location page.** In your Google profile, set
  the website link to that office's location page (or your homepage if you
  only have one office).
- **Directories → provider page.** Where Healthgrades, Psychology Today and
  others allow a website link, use the **provider's own page**, not the homepage.
- **Provider page ↔ service pages ↔ location page.** Each provider page links
  to the services they offer and the office where they practice, and each
  service and location page links back. This helps patients and search
  engines understand who does what where.
- **Footer on every page:** practice name, address, phone and hours, copied
  exactly from your fact sheet.

## Step 3: Required and recommended notices

Many practices miss these. Check each one with your compliance lead.

| Notice | Who it applies to | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **Notice of Privacy Practices** | HIPAA-covered practices with a website about their services | Post it prominently on the site (usually a footer link) |
| **Good Faith Estimate notice** (No Surprises Act) | Practices that see **uninsured or self-pay** patients. This is common for therapists. | Post a notice of the right to a Good Faith Estimate prominently on the website. CMS publishes a model notice. |
| **Nondiscrimination and language assistance notice** | Practices receiving federal financial assistance (for example, Medicaid) | Ask compliance which notice and taglines to post |
| **Crisis line** | Mental health, psychiatric and rehab sites | Put `[CRISIS LINE TEXT]` in the footer or header of every page |
| **Telehealth states** | Any practice offering telehealth | List the states where clinicians are licensed |
| **Licensing / accreditation** | Rehab facilities; licensed clinics | Some states require license numbers in advertising. Ask compliance. |

## Step 4: Accessibility (a legal deadline for many practices)

If your practice receives federal financial assistance from HHS (for example,
many practices that accept **Medicaid**), a federal rule under Section 504
requires your website to meet the accessibility standard **WCAG 2.1 Level AA**:

| Practice size | Deadline (extended in May 2026) |
|---|---|
| 15 or more employees | **May 11, 2027** |
| Fewer than 15 employees | **May 10, 2028** |

Ask your compliance lead whether this applies to you. Even if it doesn't,
accessible pages are easier for everyone to read, including search engines.

**Quick wins you can do yourself:**
- [ ] Every image has **alt text** (a short description), for example "Front
      entrance of Example Family Pediatrics with ramp." Never show patients.
- [ ] Headings are real headings (H1, H2), not just bold text
- [ ] Links say where they go ("View insurance plans"), not "click here"
- [ ] Text has strong contrast against the background
- [ ] The phone number is clickable on mobile
- [ ] Forms have labels, and the booking flow works with a keyboard alone
- [ ] Videos have captions

|---|---|
| [Federal Register: extension of compliance dates (May 11, 2026)](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/05/11/2026-09266/extension-of-compliance-dates-for-nondiscrimination-on-the-basis-of-disability-accessibility-of-web) · [HHS announcement](https://www.hhs.gov/press-room/hhs-extends-mobile-and-web-accessibility-deadline.html) | 2026-09-23 |

## Step 5: Privacy on booking and contact pages

- **Tracking code:** marketing "pixels" and tracking code from ad and social
  platforms can send information to those companies. Federal guidance on this
  was partly struck down by a court in 2024, but the risk hasn't gone away.
  Keep tracking code **off** booking, patient portal, intake and contact-form
  pages unless your compliance lead has approved it and you have the right
  agreements in place.
- **Contact forms:** don't ask for symptoms or medical history on a general
  website form. Ask only for name, contact details and preferred time, and
  send health questions to your secure patient portal or intake system.
- **Testimonials:** mental health, psychiatric and rehab sites should **not**
  post client testimonials (see `02-google-reviews/asking-for-reviews.md`).
  Other practices need the patient's **written authorization** before
  publishing anything that identifies them, and must follow FTC rules on
  endorsements. Talk to compliance first.
- **Review stars in code:** don't add review-rating markup for your own
  reviews. Google ignores "self-serving" review markup for local businesses,
  and copying reviews onto your site raises the privacy issues above.

## Copywriting specifics for your website

The basics are in `00-start-here/copywriting-playbook.md`. These rules are
specific to web pages.

### The 4 places keywords count most on a page

| Element | What it is | Rule | Example (fictional) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Title tag** | The blue headline in Google results and the browser-tab text. Set in your website builder's "SEO title" field. | Service or name + city + practice. About 50–60 characters, because Google cuts off longer titles by width. | `Sports Physicals, Springfield IL \| Example Family Pediatrics` |
| **Meta description** | The two lines under the headline in Google. Set in "SEO description." | 120–155 characters. Say who it's for and what to do next. It doesn't affect ranking, but it affects whether people click, and Google sometimes rewrites it. | `Same-week sports and school physicals for kids and teens in Springfield. Forms completed at the visit. Most insurance accepted.` |
| **H1 heading** | The big headline on the page itself | **One** per page, in plain patient words | `Sports and School Physicals` |
| **URL** | The page address | Short, lowercase, words separated by hyphens | `/services/sports-physicals` |

**URL tip:** once a page is live, don't change its address without setting
up a redirect from the old one (website builders have a "redirects" or
"URL mapping" setting). Otherwise links from Google and directories break.

### One page, one main topic
- Each service page targets **one** main service. Put its patient phrase in
  the title tag, H1 and first paragraph, then write naturally.
- Mention the clinical term once so both kinds of searchers find you
  ("sports physical (pre-participation physical exam)").
- **Google has said there's no ideal word count.** Write enough to answer
  patients' real questions (typically a few hundred words for a service
  page), then stop.

### Hyperlocal on your website
- **Location pages** carry the local detail: parking, transit, entrance
  photo, landmarks, which providers are there and when, and an embedded map.
- **Each location page must be genuinely different.** If two office pages
  differ only in the address, rewrite them.
- **Telehealth:** one page saying which states you serve, not one page per city.
- Mention your city on service pages once or twice (title tag and one
  sentence). There's no need to repeat it.

### Tone and voice
- Speak to the reader as "you." Websites allow a little more warmth than
  directories.
- Follow the tone table for your practice type in the copywriting playbook.
- **Reading level: 6th–8th grade.** Short sentences, short paragraphs, clear
  subheadings. Most patients read on a phone.

### Emojis and formatting
- **No emojis** in title tags, meta descriptions, headings or body text.
- Use real bullet lists and subheadings instead. They're easier to scan and
  easier for screen readers.

### Trust signals (they help patients and search engines)
- A **"Medically reviewed by [PROVIDER NAME], [CREDENTIALS]"** line with a
  date on every service page, and a link to that provider's page.
- Real photos of your office and team, never stock photos presented as your
  staff, and never patients.
- A visible **"Last updated"** date on the insurance page.

### How often to update your website

| When | What |
|---|---|
| **Same week** | Provider joins or leaves: add or remove their page; update service and location pages |
| **Same week** | New address, phone or hours: update the footer, location page **and** contact page |
| **After every insurance contract change** | Insurance page, including its "Last updated" date |
| **Every 3 months** | Click every phone link and booking link; fix broken links |
| **Every year** | Reread service pages for accuracy and have the reviewing clinician re-date them; check keywords against your list; run an accessibility check |

## Monthly 5-minute website check

- [ ] Name, address, phone and hours in the footer match the fact sheet exactly
- [ ] Every current provider has a page, and no former provider does
- [ ] "Accepting new patients" wording is accurate
- [ ] Insurance page "Last updated" date is within the last 3 months
- [ ] Booking link and phone link work on a phone
