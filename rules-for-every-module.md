# Rules for Every Module

These rules apply to everything you write or publish with this package. If a
template ever seems to conflict with one of them, follow the rule.

## 1. Never use patient information

- Never include a patient's name, age, condition, visit, photo or story in
  any listing, reply or page. That applies even if the patient shared it
  first and even if they gave permission verbally.
- **Photos:** no patients, including the backs of heads and children in the
  waiting room. No computer screens, schedules, whiteboards or paperwork in
  the frame.
- Every example in this package is fictional. Keep it that way when you adapt it.

## 2. No outcome claims

Describe **what you offer**, not **what will happen**.

| Don't write | Write instead |
|---|---|
| "We cure anxiety" | "We offer therapy for anxiety" |
| "Guaranteed clear skin" | "Medical and cosmetic dermatology care" |
| "Proven results" / "95% success rate" | "Evidence-based treatment options, including [NAMED APPROACH]" |
| "The best pediatrician in [CITY]" | "Board-certified pediatricians in [CITY]" |
| "Get sober for good" | "Detox, residential and outpatient programs for substance use" |

Words to search for and remove before you publish: *cure, guaranteed,
proven, best, #1, success rate, permanent, painless, risk-free, miracle.*

## 3. Reviews: protect privacy in every reply

- A reply **never confirms or suggests that the reviewer is a patient.** Don't
  write "we're sorry your visit…", "when you came in…", "your son's
  appointment…" or anything about treatment, dates, insurance or bills.
- That applies **even if the reviewer said it first.** Their post doesn't
  give you permission to discuss it.
- Real enforcement examples: in 2022, federal regulators (HHS Office for
  Civil Rights) made one dental practice pay $23,000 and another $50,000 for
  disclosing patient details while replying to online reviews.
- Use the reply library in `02-google-reviews`. It's written to follow these rules.

## 4. Reviews: when you may ask

- **Mental health and psychiatric practices, and rehab/addiction programs:**
  do not ask current clients for reviews. Professional ethics codes (for
  example, APA Ethics Code 5.05 and ACA Code of Ethics C.3.b) prohibit
  soliciting testimonials from current clients, and the ACA code includes
  former clients too. Passive options are covered in
  `02-google-reviews/asking-for-reviews.md`.
- **All practices:** never offer anything in return for a review, never ask
  only happy patients, never write reviews for yourself or have staff do it,
  and never ask for specific wording or star ratings. These break Google's
  review policy, and several also break the FTC's consumer reviews rule
  (16 CFR Part 465, in effect since October 2024).

## 5. No scores or grades

This package doesn't rate or score your visibility, and you shouldn't use tools
that promise a "visibility score" as proof of anything. Use the checklists
instead: each item is either done or not done.

## 6. Check the limits

Every module shows when its character limits and rules were last checked. If
a date is more than about 6 months old, confirm on the platform before
relying on it.

## 7. Write it well

Keywords, local wording, emojis (none), tone, respectful language and the
update calendar are all in `copywriting-playbook.md` in this folder.

## 8. Get a second pair of eyes

These templates support compliance but **don't replace review** by your
compliance officer, attorney or licensing board. That matters most for
review replies and any wording about treatment.
