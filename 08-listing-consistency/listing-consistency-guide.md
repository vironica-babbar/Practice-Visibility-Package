# Listing Consistency Guide

Your practice is listed in dozens of places online, many of which you never
created. **Listing consistency** means your name, address, phone number and
hours are **identical everywhere.** Marketers call this "NAP" (Name, Address,
Phone).

**Why it matters:**
- **Patients:** a wrong phone number or old address sends a patient
  somewhere else, and many won't try again.
- **Google and Bing:** matching details across the web help confirm your
  listing is real and accurate.
- **AI assistants:** they compare sources. Conflicting details lead to wrong
  answers, or to being left out (see `06-ai-search`).

This module is the **audit** that ties the whole package together: find every
listing, compare it with your fact sheet and fix what's wrong.

**Files in this folder**

| File | What it is |

## Step 1: Lock your "official" version (15 minutes)

Before auditing, decide the **one** way each fact is written. Put it in your
fact sheet and never vary it.

| Fact | Decide… | Consistent (fictional) | Inconsistent (don't mix these) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Name** | Exact real-world name, no "LLC," no keywords | Example Family Pediatrics | Example Family Pediatrics, LLC · Example Pediatrics · Example Family Peds |
| **Street** | Abbreviations: pick one style | 123 Sample Street, Suite 200 | 123 Sample St. Ste 200 · 123 Sample St #200 |
| **Phone** | One main local number | (555) 010-0100 | a call-tracking number on some sites, an old number on others |
| **Hours** | One format, and the same hours everywhere | Mon–Fri 8:00 AM – 5:00 PM | 8–5 on one site, 8:30–4:30 on another |
| **Website** | One version of the address | https://www.example.com | http://example.com · example.com/home |

**Tip for the address:** look up your address in the **USPS ZIP Code
lookup** (tools.usps.com). The format it returns is the official postal
version. Using it everywhere avoids mismatches.

### About call-tracking numbers
Some marketing tools give you special phone numbers to count calls. **Never
use a tracking number on directories or listings.** If you use one on
Google, make your **real main number** the "additional phone" there, and use
only the real number everywhere else. If you can't manage that reliably,
skip tracking numbers.

## Step 2: Find every listing (30–45 minutes)

Many listings exist that you never created. They were copied from other
sources. Find them with these searches on Google **and** Bing:

| Search for | Why |
|---|---|
| `"[PRACTICE NAME]" [CITY]` | Your main listings |
| `"[PHONE]"`, in quotes | Listings showing your number, including under wrong names |
| `"[OLD PHONE NUMBER]"`, in quotes | Listings that still show a number you no longer use |
| `"[OLD ADDRESS]"`, in quotes | Listings at a previous location |
| `"[PROVIDER NAME]" [CITY]` for each provider **and each former provider** | Provider listings, including ghosts of people who've left |
| `"[PRACTICE NAME]"` plus `yelp`, `healthgrades`, `facebook` | Common places listings hide |

Put every result in the tracker (`listing-audit-tracker.csv`).

## Step 3: Audit in priority order

You can't fix everything at once. Work tier by tier.

### Tier 1: fix first (most patients see these)

| Listing | Where it's covered |
|---|---|
| Google Business Profile (practice **and** every provider) | `01` |
| Apple Maps and Bing Places | `07` |
| Your website: footer, contact page, location pages | `05` |
| NPI Registry (every provider) | `03`, Step 0 |
| Insurance directories and CAQH | `04` |
| Healthgrades, WebMD and your top specialty directory | `03` |

### Tier 2: fix next

| Listing | Notes |
|---|---|
| Other specialty and society directories | `03` |
| **Yelp** | Claim your free business page at biz.yelp.com and correct the details. **Yelp prohibits asking anyone for reviews, in any form,** so don't include Yelp in any review requests. Reply to Yelp reviews using `02-google-reviews/review-reply-library.md`; the same HIPAA rules apply, including to Yelp's private messages. |
| **Facebook page "About" info** (if you have a page) | Only the name, address, phone, hours and website fields matter here. Posting and social strategy are outside this package. |
| Hospital "Find a Doctor" pages | OB/GYN and pediatrics especially (`03`) |
| Your state's treatment directory | Rehab (`03`) |

| Listing | Notes |

Foursquare lets you claim a listing for free at foursquare.com. Data Axle has an "update your listing" option. Others are often reached only through paid listing services. |
| Everything else your searches found | Small directories, old chamber of commerce pages and similar. Fix the ones showing a wrong phone or address first. |

## Step 4: How to fix each type of problem

| Problem | What to do |
|---|---|
| **Wrong details on a listing you can claim** | Claim it, verify, correct every field from the fact sheet |
| **Wrong details on a listing you can't claim** | Use the site's "suggest an edit," "report incorrect info" or contact form. Record the date in the tracker and recheck in 30 days. |
| **Duplicate Google listing** (two listings for the same practice at the same address) | If you manage both, follow Google's duplicate steps (search "resolve duplicate profiles" in Google's help center). If you don't manage the other one, use **Suggest an edit** on Google Maps and report it as a duplicate. |
| **Listing at your old address** | Don't just delete it. On Google, mark it **moved** if you can, so people are pointed to the new location. Elsewhere, update the address or report it as moved. |
| **Provider who has left** | On Google, the provider owns their listing. If they're practicing elsewhere, ask them to move it to their new location. If they've retired or died, it can be marked permanently closed. Remove them from your website, directories and insurers (`04`, Template 4). |
| **Listing for a staff member who isn't a clinician** | On Google Maps, **Suggest an edit** → mark it closed → **"never existed"** (support staff shouldn't have listings) |
| **Your name with someone else's phone number** | Report it right away on every platform where it appears. This is a known problem for **addiction treatment** facilities, where call centers take over listings. Screenshot it first. |
| **Old phone number still showing** | Keep the old number forwarding to your main line, if you can, until the listings are fixed |

### Paid listing services
Services such as Yext, BrightLocal, Moz Local and others can push your
details to many sites at once, which saves time for multi-location practices.
Before you sign:
- Ask **what happens when you cancel.** With some services, listings may
  stop being maintained or revert to earlier data.
- Keep claiming your **Tier 1** listings yourself either way, so you always
  keep control of them.
- No service can fix insurance directories or the NPI Registry for you. Those
  stay with your team.

## Practice-type specifics

| Practice type | Consistency issues to watch |
|---|---|
| Mental health | Clinicians who've left often keep listings pointing to your address and phone. Solo therapists who've moved from an office to telehealth need old office listings closed or updated. |
| OB/GYN | Hospitals often create listings for your providers **at the hospital's address.** Check that patients can tell where the office is versus where deliveries happen. |
| Dermatology | Cosmetic "med spa" brands with different names: keep each brand's listings separate and internally consistent |
| Pediatrics | Old after-hours numbers and closed satellite offices are the most common errors |
| Psychiatric | Many prescribers are part-time or move between practices. Audit provider listings every 6 months, not just yearly. |
| Rehab / addiction | Listings hijacked by call centers or referral services (see above). Your license and accreditation details should match on every listing that shows them. Report any look-alike listing. |

## Copywriting specifics for consistency

The basics are in `00-start-here/copywriting-playbook.md`.

| Topic | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Character for character** | "Suite 200" is not "Ste 200." "MD" is not "M.D." Pick one and copy-paste it; don't retype. |
| **Keep a master "copy block"** | Keep one text file with your official name, address, phone, hours, website and short description. Copy from it every time. Never type from memory. |
| **Descriptions can vary in length but not in facts** | Shorter fields get the shortened version, but the services, ages, insurance and location must never contradict each other |
| **Keywords** | Never add keywords to the name on any site, even small directories. It creates a "different name" mismatch. |
| **Hyperlocal** | Use the same neighborhood and landmark wording everywhere |
| **Emojis / symbols** | None. Some sites strip or mangle them, which creates mismatches. |

### How often

| When | What |
|---|---|
| **Same week as any change** (move, new phone, provider leaves, hours change) | Update all Tier 1 listings, then Tier 2 |
| **Every 3 months** | Quick check: Tier 1 only, about 30 minutes |
| **Every 6 months** | Full audit: all three tiers, plus the Step 2 searches for old phones, old addresses and former providers |
| **After any move or rebrand** | Full audit at 1 month and again at 3 months, since copies of old data keep resurfacing for a while |
