# Copywriting Playbook

How to write the words that go into your listings: Google, directories,
insurance listings and review replies. Each module in this package also has a
short **"Copywriting specifics"** section with the rules for that channel.
This page covers the basics they all share.

> **Scope:** this page is about the text **inside your listings**. It doesn't
> cover blogs, social media posts, newsletters or ads.

**Contents**
1. Keywords: use the words patients use
2. Hyperlocal wording: help patients picture where you are
3. Emojis and special characters
4. Tone by practice type
5. Respectful language
6. How often to update (master calendar)

---

## 1. Keywords: use the words patients use

A **keyword** is simply the words someone types into Google or a directory
search box. Patients rarely use clinical terms. A parent searches "kids
doctor," not "pediatric primary care."

### The golden rule: say it both ways, once each

Put the **patient phrase** next to the **clinical phrase** the first time
you mention a service. Then move on without repeating it.

| Instead of only… | Write… |
|---|---|
| Obstetrician-gynecologist | obstetrician-gynecologist (OB-GYN) |
| Dermatologic examination | full-body skin check (skin cancer screening) |
| Psychotherapy | therapy (counseling) |
| Medication for opioid use disorder | medication for opioid use disorder, including buprenorphine |
| Well-child visit | well-child checkups |
| Psychiatric medication management | medication management with a psychiatrist |

### Build your own keyword list (prompt for Claude)

Your keywords should come from **your** practice: the services you offer
today, your patients' words and your area. Fill in the blanks below, paste
the whole prompt into Claude, and it will build your list.

**Where to get the answers:** your fact sheet (`practice-fact-sheet.md`), your
front desk (what callers say), and the free methods in the next section.

> Don't include any patient information in the prompt. It's about your
> practice only.

```
I run a [PRACTICE TYPE, e.g. pediatric practice] in [CITY], [STATE], in the [NEIGHBORHOOD] area.

About my practice:
- We see: [WHO YOU SEE, e.g. newborns, children and teens up to age 18]
- Services we offer today: [TOP SERVICES]
- Services we do NOT offer: [SERVICES YOU DON'T OFFER]
- In network with: [INSURANCE LIST]
- Languages: [LANGUAGES]
- Care formats: [in person / telehealth for patients in STATES / both]
- Words our callers use: [PHRASES YOUR FRONT DESK HEARS, e.g. "kids doctor", "shots"]
- Search terms from our Google profile's Performance tab: [SEARCH TERMS, or "none yet"]

Please build my keyword list:
1. List 5–8 core keywords, in the words patients actually type or say, based only on the services we offer today.
2. For each keyword, give the matching clinical term, so I can use the patient phrase and the clinical phrase once each.
3. Add up to 5 narrowing phrases (insurance, language, age group, telehealth, evening hours) that are true for us.
4. Say where to use each keyword: Google services list, Google description, website page titles, directory checkboxes.
5. Show the result as a table.

Rules:
- Don't suggest anything we don't offer.
- No outcome words (cure, guaranteed, proven, best, #1).
- Don't add "near me" or lists of nearby towns.
- Don't include competitor names.
```

**When you get the list back:**
- Check every keyword against what you actually offer today. Delete anything
  that isn't true.
- Use each keyword **once or twice per field**, not in every sentence.
- Save the final table next to your fact sheet. Other modules refer to it as
  "your keyword list."

### Find your own keywords for free (15 minutes)

1. **Google autocomplete.** In a private or incognito browser window, type
   the start of a search like "pediatrician that…" or "therapist for…". Write
   down what Google suggests. Those are real searches.
2. **"People also ask."** Search your main service plus your city. Note the
   questions Google shows, because they reveal the words patients use.
3. **Your own Google profile.** In Google Business Profile, open
   **Performance**. It shows the search terms people used to find you. This
   is the best list you'll get, because it's your actual patients' words.
4. **Your front desk.** Ask staff what new callers say: "Do you do skin
   checks?" "Are you taking new babies?" Callers' wording is search wording.
5. **Google Trends** (trends.google.com, free). Compare two phrasings, like
   "OB-GYN" versus "gynecologist," in your state to see which is searched more.

### Keyword don'ts

- ❌ **Never add keywords to your business name** ("Example Pediatrics – Best
  Kids Doctor Springfield"). It's against Google's rules and can get your
  profile suspended.
- ❌ **Don't write "near me."** Google works out location from where the
  searcher is. Writing "near me" in your copy reads oddly and doesn't help.
- ❌ **Don't repeat a keyword over and over.** One or two natural mentions per
  field is enough. Repetition reads as spam to patients and to platforms.
- ❌ **Don't use keywords for things you don't offer.** Listing "EMDR" when no
  one is trained in it is misleading. It also wastes the patient's time.

## 2. Hyperlocal wording: help patients picture where you are

"Hyperlocal" means going beyond "Springfield" to the details a local person
recognizes. Patients choose care close to home, and local details build
trust quickly.

### What to include (pick 2–3 that are true)

| Type of local detail | Example (fictional) |
|---|---|
| Neighborhood | "in the Riverside neighborhood" |
| Landmark | "across from Springfield Public Library" |
| Building | "in the Oak Street Medical Building, 2nd floor" |
| Transit | "two blocks from the Main Street bus stop" |
| Parking | "free parking behind the building" |
| Hospital | "delivers at Springfield General" (OB/GYN) · "newborn visits at Springfield General" (pediatrics) |
| School or sports forms | "completes Springfield School District sports forms" (pediatrics) |
| Local insurance names | Use your state Medicaid program's real name (for example, the name printed on patients' cards), not just "Medicaid." |
| Telehealth reach | "telehealth for clients anywhere in Illinois" (only the states where you're licensed) |
| Language | Add one sentence in that language if your staff can actually serve patients in it, for example "Hablamos español." |

### Hyperlocal don'ts

- ❌ **Don't list 15 nearby towns.** Google decides who sees you partly by the
  searcher's distance from your address. You can't change that with a list,
  and long town lists look like spam. Name **one or two** communities your
  patients really come from, at most.
- ❌ **Don't write "serving [CITY]" in a city where you have no office**, unless
  you offer telehealth there and are licensed to.
- ❌ **Don't use a neighborhood name that doesn't match your address.**
  Patients will go to the wrong place.

## 3. Emojis and special characters

**Rule for this package: no emojis in any listing, bio or review reply.**

| Where | Emojis? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Business name (Google, Apple, Bing, directories) | ❌ Never | Against platform name rules |
| Google business description | ❌ | Google's guidelines forbid "gimmicky character use" |
| Review replies | ❌ | A 😊 on a reply to a medical review looks careless, and a 🙏 on a complaint can read as dismissive |
| Directory bios (Psychology Today, Healthgrades, etc.) | ❌ | Patients seeking medical or mental health care expect a professional tone |
| Insurance directories | ❌ | The fields are structured and emojis often don't display |

**Other reasons:**
- **Screen readers read emojis aloud by name.** A blind patient hears "red
  heart, sparkles, hospital" in the middle of your description.
- **Emojis can display differently** on different phones, or as empty boxes.

**Also avoid:** ALL CAPS, stacked exclamation marks ("Now open!!!"), decorative
symbols (★ ✓ ➤ ♥), and ™ or ® unless legally required.

## 4. Tone by practice type

Tone is how your writing *sounds*. The same fact can sound cold, salesy or
reassuring.

| Practice type | Aim for | Avoid | Example (fictional) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mental health | Warm, calm, non-judgmental. Speak to the reader's experience. | Clinical coldness; pressure; promises of how they'll feel | "Therapy offers a confidential space to talk openly and work on what matters to you." |
| OB/GYN | Respectful, reassuring, clear about practical facts (hospital, insurance, new patients) | Assuming every patient is pregnant or partnered; cutesy baby language | "Care for every stage, from annual exams to prenatal care and menopause." |
| Dermatology | Expert and straightforward; keep medical and cosmetic clearly separate | Beauty-industry hype ("flawless," "ageless"); before-and-after promises | "Medical care for skin, hair and nail conditions, plus cosmetic services." |
| Pediatrics | Friendly and practical, **written to parents and guardians** | Baby talk; writing to the child; fear-based messages | "Same-day sick visits so you're not left waiting when your child isn't feeling well." |
| Psychiatric | Clear, respectful, collaborative; explain what a visit involves | Implying medication is the answer for everyone; naming specific controlled medications | "We explain your options clearly so you can make informed decisions." |
| Rehab / addiction | Hopeful, non-judgmental, low-pressure; focus on next steps | Urgency and pressure ("Call NOW before it's too late"); "luxury" upselling; stigmatizing words (see section 5) | "Our admissions team can answer questions confidentially and without obligation." |

**Voice by channel:**

| Channel | Voice | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Google description | "We" (practice) | "We see children from birth to 18." |
| Healthgrades, WebMD, society directories | Third person | "Dr. Rivera cares for…" |
| Psychology Today (individual) | First person, talking to "you" | "I work with adults who…" |
| Psychology Today (group or treatment center) | "We" | "Our team provides…" |
| Review replies | "We" (practice), never a staff member's personal name | "We take all feedback seriously." |

## 5. Respectful language

Words can make people feel judged before they ever call. Use
**person-first** language: describe the person, not the condition.

| Avoid | Use instead |
|---|---|
| addict, user, alcoholic, junkie | person with a substance use disorder |
| substance abuse | substance use |
| clean / dirty (for tests or people) | negative / positive test; in recovery |
| suffering from depression | living with depression, experiencing depression |
| the mentally ill | people with mental health conditions |
| committed suicide | died by suicide |
| crazy, psycho, "OCD" as a joke | leave out entirely |
| "normal" patients | patients without [condition] |

Source: [NIDA, Words Matter](https://nida.nih.gov/research-topics/addiction-science/words-matter-preferred-language-talking-about-addiction), last checked 2026-09-23.

**Inclusive wording (all practice types):**
- Say "parents and guardians" rather than "moms" (pediatrics).
- OB/GYN: many practices use "patients" or "women and all people who need
  gynecologic care". **This is a practice decision.** Pick wording that
  reflects who you serve, then use it consistently everywhere.
- Only say "LGBTQ+ affirming," "culturally responsive" or similar if your
  team has real training and experience. Patients check.

## 6. How often to update (master calendar)

Visibility isn't one-and-done. Listings go stale when providers leave, hours
change or insurance contracts end. Put these on your calendar.

| How often | Task | Module |
|---|---|---|
| **When something changes** (provider joins/leaves, move, new phone, insurance change) | Update **every** listing within 30 days, starting with the NPI Registry and insurers | All |
| **Weekly** | Check for new reviews; reply within a few business days | 02 |
| **Monthly** | 10-minute Google profile check; test your phone number and booking link | 01 |
| **Monthly** | 20-minute AI check: ask assistants about your practice and fix wrong facts at the source | 06 |
| **Before every holiday** | Set holiday hours on Google (and Apple/Bing once set up) | 01, 07 |
| **Every 90 days** | Verify your listing in each insurer's directory (insurers must verify at least this often) | 04 |
| **Every 120 days** | CAQH ProView re-attestation for each provider (the interval can differ by state; check your account) | 04 |
| **Every 3 months** | Add 1–2 new photos to Google (new staff headshots, updated waiting room) | 01 |
| **Every 6 months** | Check the NPI Registry and specialty directories; run the checklists in each module | 03 |
| **Every year** | Reread all your copy against your keyword list; recheck the "Last checked" dates in this package | All |
| **Every spring** | Complete SAMHSA's N-SUMHSS survey (FindTreatment.gov listings only) | 03 |
